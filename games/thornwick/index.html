      Raising the realm…

      Hold the keep, take the realm

      Grow a village around your keep, beat back the rival lords who attack it, and take every rival keep to win. If your keep falls, the game is over.

      GentleEasyNormalHardBrutal

      Your first minutes

      1. Food and wood first. Build a farm and a lumber camp near the keep, then a quarry next to some rocks. Villagers take the jobs by themselves, farms first.
      2. Build cottages. New villagers arrive only when there is free room in a home and at least 20 food in store. More people means more workers and soldiers. Put a well near the houses for 2 more places each.
      3. Watch the pennant. The banner at the top counts down to the next attack. On Normal, the first one comes after about three and a half minutes. Before it runs out, put one or two watchtowers next to the keep, or ring it with a palisade. The keep shoots too. Later, a barracks trains swordsmen for gold.
      4. Rebuild the keep. Tap the keep when you have 150 wood, 120 stone and 60 gold. A stone keep holds more people and opens the windmill, tavern, stone walls, towers and more.

      Keep everyone fed

      • Each person, soldiers too, eats 1 food every 8 seconds. One farmer feeds about 4 people, a fisher about 6.
      • Farms give nothing in winter but half again in autumn. Fill your stores before the snow. Fishers still work in winter.
      • When the food runs out, villagers leave one by one.
      • A hunter's lodge sends two hunters after the deer and boar that live by the forests. They bring home meat, which people eat for up to a quarter of each meal.

      Goods and crafts

      • The Industry tab holds the workshops. Each takes villagers like any other job, after the farms, fishers, woodcutters, quarries and markets.
      • Iron: an iron mine next to rocks digs ore. A blacksmith forges it into weapons and tools. Every soldier but rams and catapults needs a weapon to train. You start with 20. Tools make farmers, fishers, woodcutters, masons, miners and shepherds a fifth faster.
      • Bread: the windmill grinds spare food into flour, and a bakery bakes it into bread. People eat bread first, for up to half of each meal.
      • Ale and cloth: a brewery brews spare food into ale for the taverns. A sheep farm shears wool and a weaver makes cloth.
      • Trees and rocks run out. A tree holds 120 to 180 wood, a rock 120 to 200 stone. Plant trees on the Decor tab, or claim new land.
      • The goods you have show in the second row of the top bar. Tap "Realm" for charts of your stores, an overview of all known lords, your villagers' needs and trade.

      Needs of the people

      • Villagers want food on the table (any three of grain, fish, bread and meat), ale at a tavern, cloth, and a chapel within 10 tiles of their home. The face in the top bar shows how much of that they have. A minstrel or a feast makes them merrier for a while.
      • Content villagers work up to a quarter faster and draw settlers up to a third faster. A village never suffers for unmet needs until it holds 40 people. From then on, while less than 40% of their needs are met, settlers come more slowly.

      Weather and the wild

      • The weather shows next to the season in the top bar. Rain helps the crops and puts out fires. Storms shorten the reach of towers and archers, and lightning can set thatch alight. A summer drought withers the fields. Deep snow in winter slows everyone off the roads, raiders too. In fog, towers see less far and raiders stay hidden until they are close.
      • Wolves come out of the woods in winter and go for lone villagers and sheep. Towers, archers and soldiers drive them off, and walls keep them out. On Gentle and Easy, and in the first winter, they only bite and scare.
      • Forests slowly spread onto open grass next to them, but never onto roads, farms or the ground around your buildings.
      • Travellers pass through: pilgrims leave gold at a chapel, minstrels cheer the village, and now and then a wandering knight offers his sword. Tap him to hire him. The lords send merchant caravans to each other, and bandits rob them on the road.

      Study and trade

      • With a stone keep, build a scriptorium. Its scholar studies one upgrade at a time, such as crop rotation, masonry or longbows. Tap it to choose.
      • Tap a market and choose "Trade routes" to send caravans to a lord who is not at war with you. They sell what you send and bring back what you ask for. Each lord sells some goods cheaply. Bandits and raiders rob caravans on the road.

      Gold

      • Markets sell your spare goods for gold. Taverns earn gold from the villagers, more when they pour ale. Every 10 villagers also pay 1 gold in tax.
      • You also get gold for each raider you kill and for each attack you beat back.
      • Gold pays for soldiers, scouts, new outposts, the keep, studies and trade.

      Defend the keep

      • Paint a ring of palisade or stone wall around the keep and put a gatehouse in it, so your villagers can still get out.
      • Towers and the keep shoot at raiders. Archers stand on stone walls by themselves. Braziers keep towers at full reach at night.
      • Pikemen are the answer to brutes, rams and catapults. Crossbowmen pierce armour. Tap any enemy to see what it is.
      • Shield-bearers hold the line in front of a banner and shelter the archers behind them from arrows.
      • Thatch burns. Keep a well within 4 tiles of thatched buildings so fires go out fast.
      • Buildings repair by themselves when no raiders are near.

      Grow and win

      • You only own the land around your keep. Build an outpost on the dim land at your border to claim that region and all the trees and rocks on it. Each outpost costs more gold than the last.
      • Tap the fog to send a scout. Some lands hold ruins, gold mines, standing stones or empty hamlets. Claim the land to get their reward.
      • Plant war banners to send your army out. Up to four can fly at once, and the army splits between them by itself, more to where the fight is bigger. Tap a banner to give its squad a fixed size, or to choose Charge or Hold. Soldiers in no squad guard home.
      • Tap a rival castle and choose "Lay siege" to plant a banner there. Rams and catapults from a siege workshop break walls fast. Engineers raise a siege tower to climb the wall, or dig a tunnel that brings it down.
      • The realm grows as you explore it. At first only the land round your keep is open, with three rival lords in it. When your scouts or outposts reach the mist at its edge, new land opens beyond. The lords push the mist back too, until the realm reaches the sea.
      • Each lord has their own character. Tap a rival keep to read about its lord. The nearest lies a good march away. New lords rise in the land that opens, with keeps and hosts as strong as the times. Up to eight rule at once.
      • The lords do not sit still. They claim land with outposts, settle it, and go to war with each other. A lord who takes another's keep takes all their land with it.
      • If Thornwick grows far stronger than any of them, the lords turn on you together. The same happens to a lord who grows too strong.
      • Your soldiers and the raiders both go for people and buildings first. They only break walls when there is no other way in. Once the realm has reached its full size, take every rival keep to win.

      Battle

      • Morale. Soldiers who see their comrades fall around them, or who are badly outnumbered, lose heart and run for a few seconds. The captain, the war cry, their own banner and home ground keep them steady. Raiders break the same way.
      • Flanks. A unit already fighting someone else, or struck from behind, takes more damage.
      • Woods and hills. Everyone moves slowly through trees, and trees catch many of the arrows shot at those among them. Archers and towers on high ground (hills lands, or next to rocks) reach one tile further.
      • The lords learn. Later in the game a rival sends a scout before an attack, then strikes from your weakest side. Kill the scout and they come in blind. Their rams look for your gates and their catapults for your towers. Larger hosts are led by a champion: cut him down and his men waver. A beaten host now breaks and flees instead of dying to the last man.

      Controls

      • Drag a building card onto the map, or tap a card and then tap tiles. Walls, moats and stakes: drag to paint a line. Cottages, farms, wells and watchtowers: drag to lay a row with a gap between.
      • "Wall ring" lays a whole ring of palisade or stone round a point, with a gatehouse. Tap any wall and choose "Copy this wall" to build the same layout somewhere else. While placing, circles show the reach of towers, wells, windmills, chapels and braziers, and what they reach.
      • Tap a building to see what it does, how well it works and what you can do with it.
      • Drag the map to move. Scroll, pinch or press + and − to zoom. Esc cancels. Use the speed buttons to pause or go faster.
      • Tap or drag on the small map in the corner to jump across the realm. Red rings mark where an attack begins, orange rings a fire.
      • The buttons under the small map jump to idle villagers (key I), workplaces with no one at work (E), fires (F) and the latest attack (A). Press again for the next one. Log (L) keeps every message.

      The game keeps no saves. Each time you open it, a new realm begins.
